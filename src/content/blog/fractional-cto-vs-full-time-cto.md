## The decision in one table

| | Fractional CTO | Full-time CTO |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Pay only for the time you need | Full salary, benefits and often equity |
| Commitment | Flexible, easy to scale up or down | Long-term hire; costly to change |
| Time to start | Days to a few weeks | Often several months to recruit |
| Experience | Senior, with patterns from many companies | Deep focus on one company |
| Availability | Scheduled, not always on call | Always available |
| Best for | Early stage, SMEs, specific problems | Technology-led companies at scale |

## When a fractional CTO is the better choice

**You are pre-product or early-stage.** Most of your risk is deciding what to build and how. That needs experience, not a large daily workload.

**Cash is tight.** Spending a full executive salary before you have revenue can shorten your runway. Part-time leadership protects it.

**You are non-technical.** You need someone on your side who can judge developers and agencies and say when a quote is unreasonable.

**You are between leaders.** If your previous CTO left, an interim fractional CTO keeps things moving while you recruit properly.

**You have a defined problem.** Migrating to the cloud, preparing for an audit, stabilising a failing product, or scaling a team are projects with a start and end.

## When a full-time CTO is the better choice

- Technology is your product and the roadmap needs daily leadership.
- You manage a large team that needs a dedicated head.
- You are raising or have raised significant capital and investors expect a full-time executive.
- The workload clearly exceeds what part-time hours can cover.

## The risks on each side

**Fractional risks:** limited availability, split attention and weaker team culture connection. Reduce them with a clear scope, a fixed weekly rhythm, a shared backlog and written decisions.

**Full-time risks:** a wrong hire is expensive and slow to fix, and a senior executive may be underused at an early stage. Reduce them by starting with a fractional engagement and testing the fit.

## A common path

Many companies follow three stages: a fractional CTO to set direction and build the first team, a longer hands-on engagement as the product grows, and a full-time CTO once the workload and funding justify it. The fractional CTO often helps hire their own successor.

## What to ask before you decide

1. What are the three biggest technology risks in the next six months?
2. How many hours a week would they realistically take to manage?
3. Is the budget better spent on a CTO or on engineers?
4. What will make us ready for a full-time hire?
