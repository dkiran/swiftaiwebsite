## Start with the role, not the person

Before you meet candidates, write down what the CTO must own in the next 12 months. For most early companies it is some mix of: choosing and building the first product, hiring the first engineers, managing vendors, keeping data secure and keeping costs under control. If you cannot describe the outcome you want, you cannot judge whether someone delivered it.

## Choose the arrangement

**Co-founder CTO.** Equity-heavy, long-term, high alignment. Right if you find someone you trust deeply and the business justifies full commitment.

**Full-time employee CTO.** Salary plus smaller equity. Right once you have funding and a substantial roadmap.

**Fractional CTO.** Part-time, flexible, lower cost, easy to adjust. Right when you are validating an idea, working with a small team, or unsure of your needs. See our comparison of [fractional and full-time CTOs](/blog/fractional-cto-vs-full-time-cto/).

## What to look for

- **Judgement over cleverness.** Can they choose a simple solution and say no to unnecessary complexity?
- **Plain-language communication.** If they cannot explain a trade-off to you, they will struggle with your investors and customers.
- **Business awareness.** They should ask about customers, revenue and risks, not only technology.
- **Track record.** Products shipped, teams built and problems solved, with references.
- **Honesty about unknowns.** The best candidates say what they would need to find out.

## Questions worth asking

1. Tell me about a technical decision you regretted. What did you learn?
2. How would you build the first version of our product in eight weeks?
3. How do you decide between building and buying?
4. How would you tell me a deadline will slip?
5. What would make you leave a company like ours?

## Structure the deal clearly

Agree these in writing: scope of ownership, working hours or time commitment, compensation, equity and vesting (if any), intellectual property assignment to the company, confidentiality and how either side can exit. Do not skip vesting or IP assignment. Take legal advice before signing.

## Managing the relationship

- **Set outcomes, not tasks.** "Launch the pilot with 50 users by March" beats "use framework X".
- **Meet weekly.** Review progress, risks and decisions in a fixed rhythm.
- **Ask for documentation.** Decisions, accounts, repositories and credentials must belong to the company.
- **Keep an independent check.** A periodic review by another expert protects both sides.

## Protect yourself early

Make sure the company, not any individual, owns the code, domains, cloud accounts and customer data from day one. This is simple to arrange early and painful to fix later.
