## The short definition

**A fractional CTO is a senior technology leader who works for your company for a fixed part of their time rather than full-time.** They do what a CTO does: set technical strategy, choose architecture, build and guide the engineering team, manage vendors and make sure technology serves the business. You simply buy a portion of their week.

## Why the model exists

A strong CTO is expensive and, for an early-stage company, often more than you need on day one. A founder with a product idea still needs sound technical decisions: which stack, build or buy, how to hire, how to avoid a rebuild in a year. Making those decisions badly is far more costly than paying for part-time expertise. Fractional leadership fills the gap.

## What a fractional CTO actually does

- **Strategy:** translates business goals into a technology roadmap and budget.
- **Architecture:** chooses a design that works today and does not trap you tomorrow.
- **Team:** hires, mentors and manages engineers, or evaluates and oversees an outsourced team.
- **Delivery:** sets up process, quality checks and releases so software ships reliably.
- **Risk:** reviews security, data protection, vendor lock-in and cost.
- **Investor readiness:** prepares the technical story and handles due diligence questions.

## Signs you need one

1. You are a non-technical founder relying on freelancers or an agency and cannot judge their work.
2. You have developers but nobody setting direction, so projects drift.
3. Cloud and tool costs are rising without clear reasons.
4. A launch or fundraising round is coming and you need confidence in the technology.
5. You are about to hire a full-time CTO and want help defining the role and interviewing.

## What it costs

Fees depend on the hours per week, the seniority of the person and whether the role is advisory or hands-on. A fractional model typically costs a fraction of a full-time CTO's total compensation and carries no long-term commitment. Use our [fractional CTO cost calculator](/fractional-cto/cost-calculator/) for an indicative range for your situation.

## How an engagement starts

Good engagements begin with a short discovery: understand the business, the product and the team, review what exists, then agree priorities. In the first month you should receive a clear picture of risks, a roadmap tied to business outcomes and an agreed rhythm of meetings and reports.

## What a good fractional CTO is not

They are not a body-shop developer, a rubber stamp or a permanent fixture you can never replace. A good one documents decisions, builds your team's capability and is happy to hand over to a full-time CTO when the time comes.
