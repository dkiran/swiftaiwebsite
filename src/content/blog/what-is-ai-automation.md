## AI automation in plain words

**AI automation is software that uses artificial intelligence to carry out repetitive business tasks without a person doing each step.** Think of replying to routine customer questions, reading an invoice and entering it into your accounts, or following up every sales enquiry within minutes.

Businesses have automated for decades. What changed is the kind of work software can now handle. Earlier tools needed clean, structured input: a fixed form, a spreadsheet column, a button click. Modern AI can read an email written in casual Hinglish, understand a scanned bill, summarise a long document or decide which team should handle a request. That opens up the messy, human-looking work that fills most small business days.

## How AI automation differs from traditional automation

| | Traditional / rule-based | AI automation |
|---|---|---|
| Input | Structured data, fixed formats | Emails, chats, PDFs, images, free text |
| Logic | If this, then that | Understands context, handles variation |
| Setup | Every rule written by hand | Instructed in plain language, then tested |
| Breaks when | Anything changes format | Rarely, but needs monitoring and review |
| Best for | Predictable, repeating steps | Judgement-light work with variation |

The two work best together. Rules handle the predictable parts and AI handles the parts that vary.

## Where Indian SMEs usually start

Across small and mid-sized businesses, the same areas come up again and again:

- **Customer support:** answering common questions on WhatsApp, email and website chat, and handing complicated cases to a person with the full conversation attached.
- **Sales follow-up:** replying to new enquiries instantly, qualifying them, booking calls and nudging leads that went quiet.
- **Finance operations:** reading supplier invoices, matching payments, sending polite reminders for overdue amounts.
- **HR and recruiting:** screening resumes, scheduling interviews, answering candidate and employee questions.
- **Reporting:** collecting numbers from several tools into a clear daily or weekly summary.

## How to choose your first automation

A good first project has four traits:

1. **High volume.** It happens many times a week, so small savings add up.
2. **Low risk.** A mistake is easy to catch and cheap to fix.
3. **Clear success measure.** You can count hours saved, response time or errors.
4. **Data you already have.** The emails, documents or records exist and are accessible.

Avoid starting with the most complicated, politically sensitive process. Win with a smaller one, then build confidence.

## What AI automation costs and returns

Cost depends on the process, the tools involved and how much integration is needed. The return is easier to estimate than most people expect: multiply the number of tasks by the minutes each takes, by the hourly cost of the person doing them, by the share you can realistically automate. ## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Automating a broken process.** Fix the process first, otherwise you just do the wrong thing faster.
- **Removing humans entirely.** Keep review on anything involving money, legal commitments or sensitive customers.
- **Skipping measurement.** Without a before and after number, you cannot tell whether it worked.
- **Buying tools before understanding the problem.** Start with the work, not the technology.
